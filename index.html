How to spend the next 3 days

The book says about 140 hours of study. You have about 30, so skip reading it cover to cover. Spend your time on the "be able to calculate / analyse" learning objectives: they are predictable, they come up in every paper, and once you know the method you get them right. Read the "know" objectives quickly using this kit and the book's chapter summaries.

Formula card

Each one is as the workbook states it, with the chapter section in brackets. Make sure you can do every example on the onscreen calculator.

Theory and risk (Ch 1, Ch 2)

CAPM (1 §10.3)
E(R) = Rf + β × (Rm − Rf)
Rf 3%, Rm 9%, β 1.4 → 3 + 1.4×6 = 11.4%
Return in home currency (2 §12.2)
Rd = (1 + Rf)(1 + Rfx) − 1
Rf = return on the overseas asset, Rfx = currency return
Maximum drawdown (2 §3.2.3)
largest peak-to-trough fall ÷ peak
100 → 140 → 105: 35/140 = 25%
VWAP (2 §11.5)
Σ(price × volume) ÷ Σ volume
Best used for intraday trading

Accounts and ratios (Ch 3)

Capital employed
Total assets − Current liabilities + Short-term borrowing
or Shareholders' funds + Non-current liabilities + ST borrowings
ROCE
PBIT ÷ Capital employed
Asset turnover
Revenue ÷ Capital employed
Margins
Gross = Gross profit ÷ Revenue Net = Net profit ÷ Revenue
Equity multiplier
Total assets ÷ Shareholders' equity
DuPont ROE
Net margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier
EVA
NOPAT − (Invested capital × WACC)
Positive means value created
WACC
E/V × Re + D/V × Rd × (1 − Tc)
Use market values; debt is tax-adjusted
Current ratio
Current assets ÷ Current liabilities
Quick (acid test)
(Current assets − Inventory) ÷ Current liabilities
Z-score
>3.0 safe · 1.8–3.0 grey · <1.8 distress · negative = insolvency imminent
Two-year horizon
Cash cycle
Receivable days + Inventory days − Payable days
Receivables ÷ Revenue ×365; Inventory ÷ COGS ×365; Payables ÷ Purchases ×365
Gearing (debt/equity)
(Interest-bearing debt + Prefs) ÷ Ordinary equity
Interest cover
PBIT ÷ Interest payable
NPV / IRR
PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)^n IRR ≈ r1 + [NPV1 ÷ (NPV1 − NPV2)] × (r2 − r1)
IRR is the rate where NPV = 0
EBIT / EBITDA
EBIT = Net income + Interest + Tax EBITDA = EBIT + Depreciation + Amortisation

Equities and corporate actions (Ch 4)

EPS
Profit to ordinary shareholders ÷ No. of shares
P/E
Share price ÷ EPS
Historic uses last EPS, prospective uses forecast EPS
Dividend yield / cover
Yield = DPS ÷ Price Cover = EPS ÷ DPS
Price to book
Share price ÷ NAV per share
Gordon growth
P = D1 ÷ (r − g), D1 = D0 × (1 + g)
D0 10p, g 4%, r 9% → 10.4 ÷ 0.05 = 208p
TERP (rights)
(Old shares × price + New × subscription) ÷ Total shares
Rights premium = TERP − subscription price
Bonus / consolidation
Value before ÷ shares after
1-for-4 bonus at 500p → 400p; 1-for-5 consolidation at 20p → 100p

Bonds and derivatives (Ch 4)

Flat yield
Coupon ÷ Clean price
GRY (approx.)
Flat yield + [(Par − Price) ÷ Years] ÷ Price
NRY (approx.)
Flat yield × (1 − tax) + [(Par − Price) ÷ Years] ÷ Price
Real return
Simple: Nominal − Inflation Fisher: (1 + N) ÷ (1 + I) − 1
5% and 2% → 2.94%
Macaulay duration
Σ(PV of CF × t) ÷ Σ PV of CF
Zero coupon: duration = maturity
Modified duration
MD = Macaulay ÷ (1 + GRY) %ΔP ≈ −MD × Δyield
Accrued interest
Nominal × (Coupon ÷ n) × (Days accrued ÷ Days in period)
Dirty = Clean + Accrued (ex-div: accrued is negative)
Conversion premium
CB price ÷ (Conversion ratio × Share price) − 1
Index-linked coupon
Coupon × (RPI lagged ÷ RPI base)
Lag: 8 months before Sept 2005, 3 months after
Delta
Δ premium ÷ Δ underlying
ATM call +0.5, ATM put −0.5, deep ITM ±1, far OTM 0
Quality spread differential
Fixed-rate spread − Floating-rate spread
This is the total gain the swap can share

Statistics and performance (Ch 5, Ch 6)

Rental yield
Gross = Rent ÷ Value Net = (Rent − Costs) ÷ Value
Trust discount
(NAV − Price) ÷ NAV
Geometric mean
[(1+r1)(1+r2)…(1+rn)]^(1/n) − 1
Always ≤ arithmetic mean
Variance / SD
Population: Σ(x−μ)² ÷ n Sample: Σ(x−x̄)² ÷ (n−1) SD = √variance
Correlation / covariance
ρ = Cov ÷ (σA × σB) Cov = ρ × σA × σB
Normal distribution
±1σ 68.26% · ±2σ 95.5% · ±3σ 99.75%
HPR
(D + Ve − Vs) ÷ Vs
MWRR (approx.)
(V1 − V0 − net inflows) ÷ (V0 + Σ CF × months left ÷ 12)
Withdrawals are negative cash flows
TWRR
(1+R1)(1+R2)…(1+Rn) − 1
Break the period at each cash flow. GIPS requires TWRR
Sharpe
(Rp − Rf) ÷ σp
Treynor
(Rp − Rf) ÷ βp
Jensen's alpha
Rp − [Rf + βp(Rm − Rf)]
Positive plots above the SML
Attribution
Allocation = (fund weights × benchmark returns) − benchmark return Selection = actual return − (fund weights × benchmark returns)

Practice quiz

These questions are written for this kit to match the syllabus objectives and the workbook's style. They are not real CISI exam questions. Each explanation gives the chapter and section to go back to.

Traps and must-know facts

EMH. Weak form: technical analysis cannot beat the market. Semi-strong: neither technical nor fundamental analysis can. Strong: nobody can, even with inside information. Insider dealing laws make strong form impossible. The best evidence for semi-strong is that few active managers beat the market consistently.
Three different risks. Systematic risk is market-wide and cannot be diversified away (beta measures it). Unsystematic risk is specific to a company and is removed by diversification. Systemic risk is the collapse of the whole financial system through contagion.
APT vs CAPM. APT uses several factors, each with its own beta and risk premium, and needs fewer assumptions. It still assumes the factors are uncorrelated with each other and that investors can short sell. Fama-French adds size and value to CAPM. Carhart adds momentum (MOM).
Behavioural biases. Confirmation (looking for supporting evidence), anchoring (fixing on a reference price), herding, overconfidence, gambler's fallacy ("it's due to go up"), representativeness, naïve diversification (1/n), mental accounting (separate pots), loss aversion, regret aversion, sunk cost fallacy, framing.
Research payments (MiFID II). Third-party research must be paid from the firm's own resources or from a separate research payment account (RPA) funded transparently by client charges.
Stock lending. Collateral protects the lender against both counterparty risk and market risk. The lender receives a fee and a manufactured dividend, but gives up voting while the stock is on loan.
Trading venues. OTFs trade bonds, structured products, emission allowances and derivatives, never equities. An SI is a firm dealing on its own account. Dark pools are anonymous, not pre-trade transparent, and suit block trades. OTC trading gives flexibility but more counterparty risk and less transparency.
Ranking in liquidation. Fixed charge holders, then preferential creditors, then floating charge holders, then unsecured creditors, then subordinated debt, then preference shares, then ordinary shares.
Ratios move in opposite directions. If the share price falls, P/E falls and dividend yield rises. A falling bond price raises the yield.
Duration drivers. Higher duration (more price sensitivity) comes with a lower coupon, a longer maturity and a lower yield. The duration of a zero coupon bond equals its maturity.
Yield curves. Normal: upward sloping. Inverted: short rates are above long rates, the market expects rate cuts, and recession is often signalled.
Money markets. UK T-bills are usually 91 days, issued weekly by Bank of England auction. CP runs 8 to 365 days, is unsecured and issued at a discount. CDs are negotiable and can run up to 5 years. Time deposits are not negotiable.
Depositary receipts. ADRs are in US dollars, with the depositary bank as registered holder. DR holders cannot take part in rights issues directly; the depositary sells the rights for them. Sponsored programmes have one depositary appointed by the company. A GDR listing in London needs at least £30m in value and 10% of the GDRs in public hands.
EIS vs SEIS. EIS: gross assets up to £15m before issue and £16m after, fewer than 250 employees, trading less than 7 years. SEIS: assets up to £350k, fewer than 25 employees, less than 3 years trading, under £250k raised. VCTs are listed, need HMRC approval, and pay no corporation tax on their gains.
Greeks. Gamma is highest for ATM options near expiry. Vega is positive for long calls and puts and highest ATM. Theta is negative for long options and accelerates near expiry. The VIX is the 30-day implied volatility of S&P 500 options.
Swaps. A vanilla swap exchanges fixed for floating. A basis swap exchanges floating for floating, using two different reference rates. The FX forward market is used to speculate on the interest rate differential between currencies.
Fund costs. OCF excludes performance fees and TER includes them. Neither includes entry or exit charges. Assessment of value reports are annual. A higher PTR means higher dealing costs.
Fund pricing. Open-ended funds trade at NAV. Investment trusts trade at a premium or discount to NAV. A trust being wound up usually has a small discount, because the price converges to NAV.
Offshore funds. Reporting funds: gains are taxed as capital gains (CGT). Non-reporting funds: income rolls up gross, and gains on disposal are taxed as income.
ESG bonds. Green bonds fund environmental projects, blue bonds fund ocean projects, social bonds fund social outcomes, impact bonds fund prevention and early intervention, and sustainability bonds fund a mix. SASB's materiality map shows which sustainability issues matter for each industry. GRESB is the ESG benchmark for real estate. The RICS Red Book sets valuation standards.
Skew and kurtosis. Positive skew has a long right tail, with mean above median. Negative skew has a long left tail, with mean below median. Mesokurtic (normal) kurtosis is 3, leptokurtic is above 3 (fat tails) and platykurtic is below 3. Note the workbook's wording here: it calls positive skew "leftward" while describing a right tail. Go by the tail and the mean-vs-median relationship.
Technical analysis. It assumes the market discounts everything, prices move in trends, and history repeats. Overlays (moving averages) are drawn on the price chart. Oscillators (RSI over 14 days) show overbought above 70 and oversold below 30. Primary movements last years, secondary movements months, tertiary movements days or weeks.
Other risk ratios. Information ratio = excess return over the benchmark ÷ tracking error. Sortino uses downside deviation only. Calmar = average annual return ÷ maximum drawdown over 3 years. Sterling measures how efficiently risk capital is used (hedge funds). Omega considers the whole return distribution.
Group accounts. Under 20% is a simple investment. 20 to 50%, or significant influence, is an associate (equity method). Over 50% is a subsidiary: fully consolidated, with minority (non-controlling) interests shown.
