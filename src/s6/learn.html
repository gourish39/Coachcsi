Statistics formulas

Arithmetic mean
Σx ÷ n
Geometric mean
[(1+r1)(1+r2)…(1+rn)]^(1/n) − 1
Median position
Odd n: (n + 1) ÷ 2 Even n: average of the two middle values
Range / IQR
Range = Max − Min IQR = Q3 − Q1
Variance
Population: Σ(x − μ)² ÷ n Sample: Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (n − 1)
Standard deviation
√Variance
Normal distribution
±1σ 68.26% · ±2σ 95.5% · ±3σ 99.75%
Correlation / covariance
ρ = Cov ÷ (σA × σB) Cov = ρ × σA × σB

Return formulas

Holding period return
(D + Ve − Vs) ÷ Vs
Annualise by compounding: (1 + HPR)^(12/months) − 1
MWRR (simplified)
(V1 − V0 − net inflows) ÷ (V0 + Σ CF × months left ÷ 12)
Withdrawals are negative cash flows
TWRR
(1 + R1)(1 + R2)…(1 + Rn) − 1
Each sub-period starts with the value after the cash flow
Attribution
Allocation = Σ(fund wt × bmk return) − bmk return Selection = actual return − Σ(fund wt × bmk return)

Risk-adjusted formulas

Sharpe
(Rp − Rf) ÷ σp
Treynor
(Rp − Rf) ÷ βp
Jensen's alpha
Rp − [Rf + βp × (Rm − Rf)]
Information ratio
(Rp − Rbenchmark) ÷ Tracking error
Calmar
Average annual return ÷ Max drawdown (3 yrs)

Traps the wrong answers rely on

  • Sample vs population: one wrong option nearly always uses the other divisor. Read whether the data is "a sample" or "all".
  • Median with an even count: average the two middle values. Sort the data first.
  • Geometric vs arithmetic mean: for returns over time the answer is the geometric mean, and it is always the lower of the two.
  • MWRR signs: subtract net inflows on the top line. On the bottom line, add inflows and subtract withdrawals, each weighted by months remaining.
  • TWRR: the second sub-period starts from the value after the cash flow has been added.
  • Sharpe uses standard deviation; Treynor uses beta. A question giving both expects you to choose the right one.
  • Highest Jensen's alpha = actual return − CAPM return. Don't simply pick the highest return.
  • Index funds should have an alpha of zero or near zero (MCQ 80).
  • Price-weighted indices (the Dow) give the most weight to the highest-priced shares, not the biggest companies.
  • GIPS is a standard for presenting performance, not a benchmark, and it requires TWRR.
  • RSI: above 70 is overbought, below 30 oversold. The RSI is an oscillator; a moving average is an overlay.
