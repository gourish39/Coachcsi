Equity formulas

EPS
(Net income − Pref dividends) ÷ Ordinary shares
P/E
Share price ÷ EPS
PEG = P/E ÷ growth %; below 1 is attractive
Dividend yield
Dividend per share ÷ Share price
Dividend cover
EPS ÷ Dividend per share
2× prudent · below 1.5× weak · below 1× uncovered
Price to book
Share price ÷ NAV per share
Gordon growth
P = D1 ÷ (r − g) D1 = D0 × (1 + g)
EBIT / EBITDA
EBIT = Net income + Interest + Tax EBITDA = EBIT + Depreciation + Amortisation
Convertible preference premium
(Prefs needed per ordinary × Pref price) ÷ Ordinary price − 1

Corporate action formulas

TERP
(Old shares × Price + New shares × Subscription) ÷ Total shares
Rights premium (nil-paid value)
TERP − Subscription price
Bonus or split
Old price × Old shares ÷ New shares
Consolidation
Old price × Shares combined
Scrip dividend ratio
Share price ÷ Dividend per share

Bond formulas

Flat yield
Coupon ÷ Clean price
GRY (approx.)
Flat yield + [(100 − Price) ÷ Years] ÷ Price
NRY (approx.)
Flat yield × (1 − tax) + [(100 − Price) ÷ Years] ÷ Price
Real return
Simple: Nominal − Inflation Fisher: (1 + N) ÷ (1 + I) − 1
Modified duration
Macaulay ÷ (1 + GRY) %ΔPrice ≈ −MD × Δyield
Accrued interest
Nominal × (Coupon ÷ n) × (Days accrued ÷ Days in period)
Cum-interest: add to clean · Ex-interest: deduct
Index-linked payment
Coupon × (Lagged RPI ÷ Base RPI)
Convertible bond premium
Bond price ÷ (Shares per £100 × Share price) − 1 Conversion ratio = Nominal ÷ Conversion price
Spread
Bond yield − Benchmark yield (in basis points)

Derivative formulas

Delta
Δ Premium ÷ Δ Underlying
Position delta = (±1 for long/short) × option delta
Intrinsic / time value
Call IV = Price − Strike (if positive) Put IV = Strike − Price (if positive) Time value = Premium − IV

Fixed spread − Floating spread

Traps the wrong answers rely on

  • Gordon: using D0 instead of D1. If you're told the dividend "just paid", grow it first.
  • Price changes push ratios in opposite directions. If the share price falls, P/E falls and dividend yield rises (MCQ 39).
  • The two conversion formulas run in opposite directions. A convertible bond's ratio is shares received per £100. A convertible preference share's ratio is preference shares needed for one ordinary share. Read which way the question states it.
  • Rights vs open offer: rights can be sold (renounced); an open-offer entitlement cannot. The open offer discount is capped at 10%.
  • Placing is the cheapest way to float. An offer for subscription needs a full prospectus, advertising and underwriting.
  • Preference shareholders in a liquidation get back nominal value at most, after all creditors are paid.
  • Callable bonds pay more yield and putable bonds pay less. The yield follows whoever holds the option.
  • Duration goes up with a lower coupon, longer maturity and lower yield.
  • UK debentures are secured; US debentures are unsecured.
  • Index-linked lag: 8 months for gilts issued before Sept 2005, 3 months otherwise.
  • Warrants vs convertibles: exercising a warrant leaves the bond in issue; converting a convertible replaces it.
  • Currency swaps exchange principal at the start and end; interest rate swaps never do.
  • Commercial paper runs 8 to 365 days. Time deposits are not negotiable.
Workbook errors spotted in Chapter 4. (1) Section 10.6.4 says selling rights earns "£3.50 per right". That is the subscription price; a nil-paid right is worth the premium (£0.42 in that example). (2) The variance swap example gives a pay-off of $125,000, but $1m × (225 − 100) is $125m as printed. Learn the formula and don't rely on that example's figure. (3) Section 9.1.1 calls a floating-for-floating swap a "basic" swap; the syllabus term is basis swap.
